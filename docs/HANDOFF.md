# Handoff

Last updated: 2026-11-17. Read this first when picking the project up again. Domain language is in [GLOSSARY.md](../GLOSSARY.md) and decisions are in [adr/](adr/).

## What this is

Emu the Menu is a self-hosted household app. It tracks what is in the fridge, freezer and pantry, and suggests Menus that fit calorie goals, weather, Occasions and available Stock. It ran on a Raspberry Pi and is moving to the Traquenard Server (see Hosting below). Public repo: https://github.com/BAMK74/emu-the-menu, licensed PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0.

## Done

**Design** (settled in a design interview, summarised here):

- Menu is a date range of suggested Meals. Emu generates it automatically, and you can reshuffle, interrupt or modify any Meal. Generated Meals are unreserved suggestions.
- Accepting a Meal reserves its ingredients. A "cooked" confirmation then deducts them from Stock.
- Hard rules filter Recipes first: allergies, Shopping Allowance and Cooldown. A weighted score then ranks the rest by calorie fit, weather tags, expiring Stock and Occasion fit. Weights are settings, not code.
- Weather is tag-based: Recipes carry warm, cold or any. Weather and holiday region come from one Household location setting, and both are fetched online.
- Occasions are imported holidays plus manual entries. They carry a date, a headcount, tags and an optional relaxed calorie goal.
- Each Eater has a Category (Man, Woman, Teen or Child) that sets a default calorie goal, which can be overridden. Eaters also have allergies (hard exclusions) and dislikes (soft penalties).
- Accounts: Adults and Teens get accounts, and Children are profiles managed by an adult. Adults edit shared data, and others view, mark Meals cooked and edit their own preferences. Tailscale is the outer gate.
- Stock is added through barcode scans (Open Food Facts, mapping cached), receipt and label OCR that you confirm, and manual forms. Calories per Ingredient are looked up and cached, with a manual override.
- A photo-of-fridge scan is a possible later feature behind the same provider interface as OCR. It is not recorded in the glossary or ADRs yet.
- Backups: the Server backs up the Shared Postgres off-site, and Settings offers a readable JSON export.

- `src/emu_the_menu/planner.py`: `plan(start, days, meal_types, recipes, eaters, stock, seed, settings, last_served, weather)` returns a `Menu` of `Meal`s (one per day per chosen meal type, in date order) and a shopping list. Each Meal is filled the same way. Hard rules first: the Recipe is tagged for that meal type, it passes `suitability.assess` for every Eater at the table, it is out of its Cooldown (`PlanSettings.cooldown_days`, also against `last_served` from before the Menu), and it keeps the whole Menu within the Shopping Allowance (`shopping_allowance`, Ingredients not in Stock, shared across the Menu and counted once each). Then a weighted score (`Weights`: calorie fit 3, expiring Stock 2, in Stock 1.5, weather 1, soft penalty 1.5, all settings): calorie fit is how close a serving is to each Eater's share of the day, expiring means stock that runs out within 7 days of the Meal, weather is +1 or -1 for a warm or cold Recipe on a day with known weather, and the penalty is for dislikes and meat on a low-meat diet. Ties are broken from `seed`, so the same seed gives the same Menu and a new seed reshuffles. A Meal with no Recipe carries a `reason` (no recipes tagged, none suits everyone, Cooldown, Shopping Allowance). Slots: `Slot` has six places in the day, in order (breakfast, morning snack, lunch, afternoon snack, dinner, evening snack); the three snack slots all take Recipes tagged `snack` (`Slot.meal_type`), and `plan` takes `slots`. Portions: each Eater's share of their calorie goal for that Meal, in quarter servings (at least a quarter); a day's goal is split between the chosen slots by 25% breakfast, 8% each snack, 30% lunch and 35% dinner, scaled to add up to the whole day (`SLOT_SHARES`). `Meal.total_servings` and `Meal.scale` say how much to cook. Stock is counted in amounts with expiry dates (`cooking.Pantry`, see Saved Menus). Pure, no database.
- `src/emu_the_menu/web_menu.py` with `templates/menu.html`: `GET /menu`. The plan is made from the address (`go=1`, `start`, `days` 1 to 28, repeated `meal_types` and `eater`, `allowance` 0 to 50, `cooldown` 0 to 60, `seed`), so it is repeatable and nothing is saved. `meal_types` takes the six slot names (`breakfast`, `morning-snack`, `lunch`, `afternoon-snack`, `dinner`, `evening-snack`). With no `go` it plans a week from today for breakfast, lunch and dinner for everyone (snacks are off until ticked). Stock comes from `StockRepository` (None if any of an Ingredient's stock never expires, else the latest expiry). Reshuffle is a link with `seed + 1`. Bad settings give a 400 with a message and no plan.
- `src/emu_the_menu/names.py`: `stem` and `name_has_term`, for comparing food names ignoring case and plurals and matching whole words only ("peanut" is in "peanut butter", "pine" is not in "pineapple"). Used by the recipe import and by suitability.
- `src/emu_the_menu/suitability.py`: the hard rules of Menu generation for one Recipe and one Eater, `assess(recipe, facts, eater)` giving `Suitability(blocked, cautions, soft)` with plain-text reasons that name the Ingredients. Blocked: a ticked allergen in any Ingredient; a free-text allergy word in an Ingredient's name; a food group the diet excludes; and, for an Eater with any allergy or a diet that bans food groups, any unreviewed Ingredient ("not checked: X, so it may contain something this person cannot have"). Cautions (do not block): an unreviewed Ingredient for an unrestricted Eater, and "check the labels for <allergy>" for every free-text allergy with no name match, because a name cannot show what a product hides. Soft (for ranking): dislikes found in names, and meat for a low-meat diet.
- `src/emu_the_menu/page_fetch.py`: `fetch_page(url)` for pasted web addresses, built so a pasted address can never make the Pi call something private. Only http and https on ports 80 and 443, no user name in the address; every name is resolved and refused unless every answer is a public address (`ipaddress.is_global`, which also excludes the tailnet's 100.64.0.0/10, the home network, loopback, link-local and cloud metadata addresses, and IPv4-mapped IPv6); the connection is then pinned to that checked address (so DNS cannot answer differently the second time) while TLS still verifies the host name; redirects are followed by hand (at most 4) and each is checked the same way; pages over 2 MB (also after gzip) and non-HTML answers are refused; 401, 403 and 429 say the site does not allow it. `UnsafeAddressError` and `PageUnavailableError`. The network and the resolver are injectable for tests.
- `src/emu_the_menu/recipe_import.py`: `extract_recipe(html)` reads the schema.org Recipe from the page's JSON-LD (plain, in a list, in `@graph`, or with a list `@type`) and keeps only the name, servings (1 to 50, from the yield), ingredient lines and tags (keywords and category, trimmed to what a Recipe accepts). - `src/emu_the_menu/db.py` and `src/emu_the_menu/migrations/`: the Postgres connection (ADR 0006). `EMU_DATABASE_URL` names the database; each URI gets a pool of at most 10 connections (psycopg-pool, autocommit). Repositories take a URI, or another repository's `Database` to share its connection, and give the connection back on `close()`. Writes that belong together use `Database.transaction()`. On the first connection the numbered files in `migrations/` (`001_initial.sql` is the schema as it left SQLite) are applied in order under an advisory lock and recorded in `schema_migrations`; add a new file to change the schema, never edit a shipped one. Names that were `COLLATE NOCASE` are unique on `lower(name)` in the `C` collation, so lookups use `lower(name) = lower(%s)`. The pool checks each connection before lending it, so a Postgres restart costs a reconnect, not an error page. Each web request uses one connection at most, shared by every repository it opens (`request_scope`, set by the middleware in `web.py`), and every write transaction takes one advisory lock so writes run one at a time as in SQLite; both came from a load test that found a pool deadlock and colliding forecast refreshes. ## In progress

Choices to know about: 1 tsp = 5 ml, 1 tbsp = 15 ml, 1 cup = 240 ml. `reduce_stock` uses the earliest expiry first, with no-expiry items last. Ingredient names are stored lowercased and stripped. Quantities are floats, and amounts below 1e-9 count as zero. Mixing unit families for one Ingredient raises `IncompatibleUnitError`, in both `add_stock` and `reduce_stock`.

Storage: `StockItem` has an optional `id`, which is `None` until stored. `StockRepository` runs the pure stock rules inside one transaction and writes back only the changed rows. The first add of an Ingredient fixes its base unit from the unit typed, so there is no separate create-Ingredient step. A dedicated form could replace that later. The repository takes a Postgres connection URI, or another repository's `Database` to share.

Web: `create_app(database, today)` in `web.py` uses the URI given or `EMU_DATABASE_URL`, and stops at once with a clear message if neither is set. Run locally with `EMU_DATABASE_URL=postgresql://emu:emu@localhost:5432/emu uv run uvicorn emu_the_menu.web:create_app --factory` (the README says how to get a Postgres). Each request borrows a pooled connection. There is no HTMX, no login and no "cooked" action yet, and quantity changes are a manual set.

Add form, as tried on a phone:
- Units include `bunch` and `head`, which count as whole items.
- Expiry is one row: a calendar date, or "in N days/weeks/months", or a "No expiry" checkbox. They are alternatives, and the server rejects a date plus a number. Entering "in N ..." remembers that shelf life for the Ingredient (`ingredients.shelf_life_*`), and picking the Ingredient later pre-fills it. A calendar date or "No expiry" never overwrites it. Older databases are upgraded on open.
- The Ingredient box suggests every Ingredient ever added and pre-selects its unit.
- A unit-family clash (for example chicken first entered in ml, then added in g) is a 409 warning with a one-tap fix, "Store chicken in g instead". The fix calls `repo.change_base_unit`, which keeps existing numbers (500 ml becomes 500 g) because the families cannot convert. The Set form on an item row does the same. Other bad input is a red 400 error.
- Each Ingredient also remembers the quantity and unit last typed (`ingredients.last_quantity` and `last_unit`). Picking it pre-fills the unit, and the quantity if the box is empty. `change_base_unit` forgets it. This is the "typical amount" suggestion for now, and it comes from the household's own history, not the internet.
- Household location: `settings` table (key and value) and `repo.household_country()` and `set_household_country()`, by ISO code. Settings open as a `<dialog>` overlay on every page. The overlay lives in `templates/base.html` and loads `/settings/panel` (the form and backup section, as a fragment) when opened, so pages carry no settings data, and without scripting or if the fetch fails the link goes to the `/settings` page. Saving posts `next` (the page you were on) and returns to `<next>?saved=1`, and only `_RETURN_PAGES` in `web.py` (`/` and `/eaters`) are honoured, so there is no open redirect. A new page must be added to that list to get the return behaviour. Pages share `templates/base.html`, and the form is `_settings_form.html`. The country is not used by anything yet.
- **Ingredient details (Recipes piece 2, built 2026-10-30).** The `ingredients` table gained `kcal`, `reviewed` and two tag tables (`ingredient_food_groups`, `ingredient_allergens`). `StockRepository.ingredients()` now returns `id`, `kcal`, `food_groups`, `allergens` and `reviewed` too, with `get_ingredient` and `set_ingredient_details` (which marks it reviewed, and changes nothing if any value is rejected). **`reviewed` is the safety rule: an Ingredient nobody has saved is unknown, so empty food groups and allergens mean "not checked", never "none".** Recipes must treat an unreviewed Ingredient as unknown. Changing an Ingredient's unit family clears its calories, since they are measured against the old unit, and keeps the tags. The page flags unreviewed ingredients ("Not checked yet"), shows an overview, and warns that allergen tags are set by people. Suggestions from the name are pre-ticked only for unreviewed ingredients, say so in amber, and are saved only when a person saves, so a deliberate "none" is never overridden. Older databases upgrade on open. The overview chips filter the list: `/ingredients?show=no-calories` or `?show=unchecked` (links, no scripting; the active chip clears it; counts stay overall; unknown values show all).
- **Recipes (piece 3, built 2026-10-30).** A Recipe is a name, servings, meal types, weather (any, warm, cold), free tags and ingredient lines for the whole Recipe. The Ingredients page's chips filter its list (`?show=no-calories` or `?show=unchecked`). The JSON export has a `recipes` list. A Menu only planned or saved does not start a Cooldown.
  - **Cooking uses up Stock.** `cooking.uses_for(recipe, scale, held)` gives the Recipe's amounts times servings cooked divided by the Recipe's servings, one `Use` per line (a group of alternatives uses one: the first that covers the whole amount, else the one with most in Stock, else the first). Mark cooked takes that from Stock with `StockRepository.take_available` (earliest expiry first; never fails for lack of Stock; `use_available` is its total) and keeps what Stock could not cover in `menu_meals.short`, shown as "Short in stock: carrots 100 g". Cooking never blocks. Pressing it again does nothing, so Stock is taken once. Expired stock (by today) is left alone and counts as short.
  - **Not cooked puts Stock back.** Cooking records each lot it took (`stock.TakenLot`: ingredient, base-unit amount, location, expiry; table `menu_meal_taken`). Not cooked returns exactly those lots with `StockRepository.put_back` (merged into a matching item or a new one, without changing what adding remembers) and forgets them, so pressing it again puts nothing back. Only what was really taken goes back. A Meal cooked before this existed has no record and gives nothing back. The button asks first.
  - **Amounts and expiry in planning.** `cooking.Pantry` holds Stock as (quantity, expiry) lots per Ingredient id. `plan(stock=...)` takes lots, or just a date or None for an amount that is not tracked (always enough until then, which keeps older callers working). An Ingredient is covered when what is held and unexpired on the day is at least what the Meal needs at the servings that table would eat (a group of alternatives is covered by any one member), and a chosen Meal uses its share up so the next Meal sees less. Uncovered Ingredients count once each against the Shopping Allowance and are assumed bought for the rest of the Menu. Expiring soon means usable lots that run out within 7 days.
  - **Shopping list with amounts.** `cooking.shopping_amounts`: what the Meals still to cook need less what is in Stock, Stock shared out between Meals in date order, cooked Meals ignored, stock expired by a Meal's day not counted. Shown as "carrots 1.2 kg" on the Menu page and on saved Menus (`web_menu.shopping_text`).

Cooked Meals hold nothing, and an uncooked Meal on a past day lets go. Decisions from Kevin: an accepted Menu can still be changed; the Menu accepted earlier gets the Stock. New plans on the Menu page use free Stock; an accepted Menu's page uses what is left after the Menus accepted before it; a Menu not accepted uses what no accepted Menu holds; Pick another leaves out only the Meal being replaced. Cooking takes real Stock whatever is held. `stock_lots` lives in `reserve.py`.

- **Bulk review of ingredients (built 2026-10-30).** `/ingredients/review` replaces opening ingredients one by one, and is built to make a long list short. It lists every unreviewed ingredient (most used in recipes first) in three kinds. **Plain-looking** (`ingredients.judge`: a name made only of basic plant foods and describing words, such as garlic, salt, olive oil, strawberries, jasmine rice, never a bare "oil", never with a hint of an allergen or food group; bracket notes like "(counted)" are ignored and accents are folded): one group of ticked boxes confirmed in one click and saved as reviewed with nothing ticked. **Groups that share a likely answer**: ingredients whose name suggests the same food groups and allergens (at least two) form a group such as "Probably dairy, milk (24)" with its own **Confirm the ticked ones** button, which saves those tags for the ticked members (untick a vegan version, say). **One by one**: everything left, 20 per page, with the name's suggestions pre-ticked, the reason it needs a look, optional calories and a "Looks right" box that ticks itself when anything in the row changes. On the 299 imported ingredients (after tidying) this is 145 plain (one click), 95 in 16 groups (16 clicks) and about 59 to read. **Per recipe**: `?recipe=ID` limits the page to one recipe's ingredients, which is all that is needed to make that recipe checked (on the imported recipes a recipe needs a median of 5 clicks or rows, at most 11); recipe cards, the recipe page and the Menu link to it ("you check the N ingredients"). "Need a look" is the risky filter (`?show=risky` hides the plain group). **Nothing is ever saved by itself**: an ingredient counts as reviewed only when a person confirms it. The server re-checks that ids posted as plain still look plain, skips ingredients already reviewed (never overwrites a person's review), validates everything first and saves nothing if a value is bad (the error names the ingredient and typed values stay), and rebuilds the return address from `show`, `recipe` and a saved count only. `web_ingredient_review.py` is registered before `/ingredients/{id}`, and its POST opens its own `StockRepository` because it is an async route. Typical contents the name suggests (all only hints): flour, oats, granola, muesli, cereal, crackers and pasta shapes mean gluten (not cornflour, rice, almond or coconut flour, nor "gluten-free"); wine, vinegar, balsamic, sherry and dried fruit mean sulphites; worcestershire means fish; mayonnaise means eggs (not vegan); stock, broth and bouillon mean celery; hummus means sesame; hard cheeses mean milk. - **One food, one ingredient, plain names (built 2026-10-30).** `names.name_key(name)` is what two spellings of one food share: case, hyphens, plurals, describing words (sea, kosher, fine, finely, flaky, table, coarse, fresh, freshly, cracked, chopped, diced, sliced, minced, grated, shredded, crushed, roughly, thinly, lightly, organic, ripe, raw, large, small, medium, extra, virgin; "ground" for spices but not for minced meat; "grain" after fine or coarse), a seasoning pair counting as salt ("salt and pepper", "sea salt and black pepper"), and a bare "pepper" meaning black pepper. Colours, "dried" and container words (can, tin, jar) are kept, so red and black pepper, dried and fresh herbs, and tinned and fresh tomatoes stay apart. `names.clean_name(name)` is the plain name stored: describing words dropped, every salt is "salt", every black pepper "black pepper", hyphens kept in real compounds ("gluten-free", "all-purpose") and removed where they join a describing word ("sea-salt", "extra-virgin"). `canonical_name(name, known, family)` reuses a known ingredient with the same key and the same kind of unit (weight, volume or count), else uses the clean name. `StockRepository.add`, `use` and `change_base_unit`, `RecipeRepository` saves and the recipe import all go through it, so inputs are cleaned and a variant never creates a twin. For names already stored: `StockRepository.tidy_plan()` lists the groups (same key and unit kind) with the plain name each would get, `tidy()` carries it out, and `merge_ingredients(keep_id, drop_id)` does one merge (recipe lines and stock move over, quantities add up where a recipe had both, allergens and food groups of both are kept as the safer union, calories keep the kept one's else the other's, reviewed if either was, then the duplicate is deleted; different kinds of unit raise `IncompatibleUnitError`). A rename is skipped if its target name is already used by an ingredient in another kind of unit, so two groups never end up with one name. The Ingredients page shows "N ingredient names can be tidied" with a **See what would change** list and a **Tidy the names** button (`POST /ingredients/tidy`, registered before `/ingredients/{id}`): a person presses it, nothing is tidied by itself. On the 322 imported ingredients it plans 40 changes that take the list to about 300, for example salt, sea salt, fine salt, kosher salt, salt and pepper and sea salt and black pepper all become "salt", and black pepper with freshly cracked black pepper becomes "black pepper" (ground black pepper stays apart there because it is stored by volume).

- **Alternatives: "water or vegetable broth" is two ingredients (built 2026-11-02).** A recipe line that offers a choice is stored as one ingredient per alternative, all with the same quantity and the same `recipe_lines.alt_group` number (column added to older databases on open). `names.split_alternatives(name)` reads the choice: "water or vegetable broth", "almond butter or peanut butter" and "basil and/or microgreens" keep both sides as written; "brown or green lentils", "sunflower or vegetable oil", "red or white wine vinegar" and "all-natural salted peanut or almond butter" are two kinds of one food, so the shared ending is added to each (only when both sides start or end with a kind word such as a colour, a nut or an oil type); names with a slash ("chicken stock / broth") are one thing. `RecipeRepository` splits every line on save, so manual entry, the import and the edit form all work: the form shows a group as one row ("water or vegetable broth") and saving it again keeps the group. Any one alternative may be used, so `recipes.analyse` counts the allergens, food groups and unreviewed ingredients of ALL alternatives (a recipe is not checked until every alternative is) but only the highest calories of a group once; the planner treats a group as one need that any ingredient in stock meets, and a group with none in stock costs one purchase (the first) against the Shopping Allowance. The new ingredients are unreviewed, a name that lists alternatives is never "plain" and never part of a confirmable group in the bulk review, and the export has `alternative_group`. **The either/or lives in the recipe only**: ingredients are always single foods. `StockRepository` splits any stored ingredient whose name offers a choice by itself whenever the database is opened (`auto_split`, a quick check for " or " and "and/or" in names, so no button is needed), and adding stock under a choice ("water or vegetable broth") is refused with a message, because a choice between ingredients belongs in a recipe. For ingredients already stored under a combined name: `StockRepository.split_plan()` lists them and `split_ingredients()` (run on open and by `tidy()`, which now repeats until nothing changes, at most three passes) replaces each by its parts in every recipe, keeping the quantity; **what was ticked on the combined name is deliberately not copied, so every part starts unchecked** unless it already existed and a person checked it; a part whose name is used by an ingredient in another kind of unit gets a suffix like "basil (by volume)"; a combined name that stock still holds is kept. The Ingredients page's tidy note would count any split still pending under "See what would change" (normally none, since the split happens on open). On the 39 imported recipes this splits 29 names into 57 alternative lines.
- **Ingredient names ignore how much and how prepared (built 2026-11-05).** `names.strip_quantity` drops words that only say how much: "a drizzle of olive oil", "a handful of basil", "a good grind of black pepper", "a clove of garlic", "a can of tomatoes" (amount and container words before "of", with an optional article and adjective such as "large handful of"), and "zest of 1 lemon" or "juice of half a lemon" become the fruit. A measured "1 tsp lemon zest" stays its own ingredient, because a lemon is counted and zest is not. More describing words are ignored (peeled, deseeded, trimmed, washed, halved, quartered, cubed, torn, beaten, melted, softened, boneless, skinless, good quality, pitted, higher welfare); words that make a different food stay (frozen peas, cooked rice, gluten free flour, soft brown sugar, cream of tartar). `clean_name` and `name_key` both use it, so saved ingredients under the old wording are tidied by the existing **Tidy the names** button or `StockRepository.tidy()`; this was run on the Pi database on 2026-11-06 after a backup (`/mnt/emu/backups/emu-before-tidy-20261106-212256.db`): 7 changes, 318 ingredients became 317 ("few sprigs of marjoram", "higher-welfare lean pork", "melted coconut oil", "pitted and quartered kalamata olives", "pitted nicoise" and "skinless higher-welfare chicken breasts" were renamed, and "freshly cracked black pepper" merged into one ingredient). That one is still named "good grind of black pepper" (stored by weight) because "black pepper" already exists stored by volume, so a person should rename it, for example "black pepper (by weight)". Tests are in `tests/test_quantifier_names.py`.
- **Non-ingredient lines are left out at import (built 2026-11-06).** `IngredientLine.is_ingredient` is False for a line that points to another recipe (the name has the word "recipe": "base recipe", "recipe pico de gallo") or only names a category ("optional seasonings", "toppings of choice", "spices", "any extra garnishes"); real foods that share a word stay (pumpkin spice, seasoning salt, chilli flakes). The import form leaves those lines out and lists them under "Left out because they are not ingredients" so a person can add a row by hand. `_clean_name` also drops "of choice" and a trailing reason ("milk of choice for a very thick consistency" is milk; "flour for the dusting" is flour). Only new imports change: recipes already saved keep their lines (on the Pi that is "base recipe", "optional seasonings", "recipe pico de gallo" and "milk of choice for a very thick consistency", which a person can clear from those recipes).
- **The Kinetic Type look (built 2026-11-11, version C of the design exploration).** `static/look.css` holds it: magazine type (huge accent headline, uppercase labels, rules instead of boxes, flat square buttons and fields), the season colours in light and a dark variant (the device setting decides, as before; every accent is at least 4.5:1 on the page), the banner and the movement. `base.html` keeps the neutrals (`--paper`, `--ink`) and the safety colours, which never change with the season. **Season:** `look.py` picks it from today's date and the hemisphere (the weather town's latitude, else the country: AR, AU, BR, CL, NZ, ZA are southern), with Halloween (24 to 31 October) and Christmas (15 to 26 December) as overlays; Settings has a **Look** section to pin a season or turn festive colours off (`settings` keys `look_season`, `look_festive`). Every page's `<html>` carries `data-season` and `data-festive` (a context processor in `templating.py`, using `app.state.today`). **Banner:** server-rendered on every page; the Stock, Menu and saved Menus pages get the full one (line split into word spans, a sentence, a **Plan this week's menu** button except on /menu, six real produce photos in round tiles), other pages a single line.
