Last updated: 2026-10-26. Read this first when picking the project up again. Domain language is in [GLOSSARY.md](../GLOSSARY.md) and decisions are in [adr/](adr/).

## What this is

Emu the Menu is a self-hosted household app. It tracks what is in the fridge, freezer and pantry, and suggests Menus that fit calorie goals, weather, Occasions and available Stock. It runs on a Raspberry Pi and is reached over Tailscale. Private repo: https://github.com/BAMK74/emu-the-menu, licensed PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0.

## Done

**Design** (settled in a design interview, summarised here):

- Menu is a date range of suggested Meals. Emu generates it automatically, and you can reshuffle, interrupt or modify any Meal. Generated Meals are unreserved suggestions.
- Accepting a Meal reserves its ingredients. A "cooked" confirmation then deducts them from Stock.
- Hard rules filter Recipes first: allergies, Shopping Allowance and Cooldown. A weighted score then ranks the rest by calorie fit, weather tags, expiring Stock and Occasion fit. Weights are settings, not code.
- Weather is tag-based: Recipes carry warm, cold or any. Weather and holiday region come from one Household location setting, and both are fetched online.
- Occasions are imported holidays plus manual entries. They carry a date, a headcount, tags and an optional relaxed calorie goal.
- Each Eater has a Category (Man, Woman, Teen or Child) that sets a default calorie goal, which can be overridden. Eaters also have allergies (hard exclusions) and dislikes (soft penalties).
- Accounts: Adults and Teens get accounts, and Children are profiles managed by an adult. Adults edit shared data, and others view, mark Meals cooked and edit their own preferences. Tailscale is the outer gate.
- Stock is added through barcode scans (Open Food Facts, mapping cached), receipt and label OCR that you confirm, and manual forms. Calories per Ingredient are looked up and cached, with a manual override.
- A photo-of-fridge scan is a possible later feature behind the same provider interface as OCR. It is not recorded in the glossary or ADRs yet.
- Backups: a nightly SQLite copy plus a manual JSON export.

- `src/emu_the_menu/planner.py`: `plan(start, days, meal_types, recipes, eaters, stock, seed, settings, last_served, weather)` returns a `Menu` of `Meal`s (one per day per chosen meal type, in date order) and a shopping list. Each Meal is filled the same way. Hard rules first: the Recipe is tagged for that meal type, it passes `suitability.assess` for every Eater at the table, it is out of its Cooldown (`PlanSettings.cooldown_days`, also against `last_served` from before the Menu), and it keeps the whole Menu within the Shopping Allowance (`shopping_allowance`, Ingredients not in Stock, shared across the Menu and counted once each). Then a weighted score (`Weights`: calorie fit 3, expiring Stock 2, in Stock 1.5, weather 1, soft penalty 1.5, all settings): calorie fit is how close a serving is to each Eater's share of the day, expiring means stock that runs out within 7 days of the Meal, weather is +1 or -1 for a warm or cold Recipe on a day with known weather, and the penalty is for dislikes and meat on a low-meat diet. Ties are broken from `seed`, so the same seed gives the same Menu and a new seed reshuffles. A Meal with no Recipe carries a `reason` (no recipes tagged, none suits everyone, Cooldown, Shopping Allowance). Slots: `Slot` has six places in the day, in order (breakfast, morning snack, lunch, afternoon snack, dinner, evening snack); the three snack slots all take Recipes tagged `snack` (`Slot.meal_type`), and `plan` takes `slots`. Portions: each Eater's share of their calorie goal for that Meal, in quarter servings (at least a quarter); a day's goal is split between the chosen slots by 25% breakfast, 8% each snack, 30% lunch and 35% dinner, scaled to add up to the whole day (`SLOT_SHARES`). `Meal.total_servings` and `Meal.scale` say how much to cook. Stock is counted in amounts with expiry dates (`cooking.Pantry`, see Saved Menus). Pure, no database.
- `src/emu_the_menu/web_menu.py` with `templates/menu.html`: `GET /menu`. - `src/emu_the_menu/names.py`: `stem` and `name_has_term`, for comparing food names ignoring case and plurals and matching whole words only ("peanut" is in "peanut butter", "pine" is not in "pineapple"). Used by the recipe import and by suitability.
- `src/emu_the_menu/suitability.py`: the hard rules of Menu generation for one Recipe and one Eater, `assess(recipe, facts, eater)` giving `Suitability(blocked, cautions, soft)` with plain-text reasons that name the Ingredients. Blocked: a ticked allergen in any Ingredient; a free-text allergy word in an Ingredient's name; a food group the diet excludes; and, for an Eater with any allergy or a diet that bans food groups, any unreviewed Ingredient ("not checked: X, so it may contain something this person cannot have"). Cautions (do not block): an unreviewed Ingredient for an unrestricted Eater, and "check the labels for <allergy>" for every free-text allergy with no name match, because a name cannot show what a product hides. Soft (for ranking): dislikes found in names, and meat for a low-meat diet.
- `src/emu_the_menu/page_fetch.py`: `fetch_page(url)` for pasted web addresses, built so a pasted address can never make the Pi call something private. ## In progress

Nothing. Slice 1 is done, including SQLite storage and the Stock web forms, with `uv run pytest` passing all 1490 tests on Python 3.13 and `ruff` clean. Claude wrote the implementations at Kevin's request.

Choices to know about: 1 tsp = 5 ml, 1 tbsp = 15 ml, 1 cup = 240 ml. `reduce_stock` uses the earliest expiry first, with no-expiry items last. Ingredient names are stored lowercased and stripped. Quantities are floats, and amounts below 1e-9 count as zero. Mixing unit families for one Ingredient raises `IncompatibleUnitError`, in both `add_stock` and `reduce_stock`.

Storage: `StockItem` has an optional `id`, which is `None` until stored. `StockRepository` runs the pure stock rules inside one transaction and writes back only the changed rows. The first add of an Ingredient fixes its base unit from the unit typed, so there is no separate create-Ingredient step. A dedicated form could replace that later. The repository takes a database path, and nothing points at `/mnt/emu/db` yet.

Web: `create_app(db_path, today)` in `web.py` reads `EMU_DB` (default `emu.db`). Run locally with `uv run uvicorn emu_the_menu.web:create_app --factory`, optionally with `--host <tailscale ip>` to try it on a phone. Each request opens its own repository. There is no HTMX, no login and no "cooked" action yet, and quantity changes are a manual set.

Add form, as tried on a phone:
- Units include `bunch` and `head`, which count as whole items.
- Expiry is one row: a calendar date, or "in N days/weeks/months", or a "No expiry" checkbox. They are alternatives, and the server rejects a date plus a number. Entering "in N ..." remembers that shelf life for the Ingredient (`ingredients.shelf_life_*`), and picking the Ingredient later pre-fills it. A calendar date or "No expiry" never overwrites it. Older databases are upgraded on open.
- The Ingredient box suggests every Ingredient ever added and pre-selects its unit.
- A unit-family clash (for example chicken first entered in ml, then added in g) is a 409 warning with a one-tap fix, "Store chicken in g instead". The fix calls `repo.change_base_unit`, which keeps existing numbers (500 ml becomes 500 g) because the families cannot convert. The Set form on an item row does the same. Other bad input is a red 400 error.
- Each Ingredient also remembers the quantity and unit last typed (`ingredients.last_quantity` and `last_unit`). Picking it pre-fills the unit, and the quantity if the box is empty. `change_base_unit` forgets it. This is the "typical amount" suggestion for now, and it comes from the household's own history, not the internet.
- Household location: `settings` table (key and value) and `repo.household_country()` and `set_household_country()`, by ISO code. Settings open as a `<dialog>` overlay on every page. The overlay lives in `templates/base.html` and loads `/settings/panel` (the form and backup section, as a fragment) when opened, so pages carry no settings data, and without scripting or if the fetch fails the link goes to the `/settings` page. Saving posts `next` (the page you were on) and returns to `<next>?saved=1`, and only `_RETURN_PAGES` in `web.py` (`/` and `/eaters`) are honoured, so there is no open redirect. A new page must be added to that list to get the return behaviour. Pages share `templates/base.html`, and the form is `_settings_form.html`. The country is not used by anything yet.
- **Ingredient details (Recipes piece 2, built 2026-10-30).** The `ingredients` table gained `kcal`, `reviewed` and two tag tables (`ingredient_food_groups`, `ingredient_allergens`). `StockRepository.ingredients()` now returns `id`, `kcal`, `food_groups`, `allergens` and `reviewed` too, with `get_ingredient` and `set_ingredient_details` (which marks it reviewed, and changes nothing if any value is rejected). **`reviewed` is the safety rule: an Ingredient nobody has saved is unknown, so empty food groups and allergens mean "not checked", never "none".** Recipes must treat an unreviewed Ingredient as unknown. Changing an Ingredient's unit family clears its calories, since they are measured against the old unit, and keeps the tags. The page flags unreviewed ingredients ("Not checked yet"), shows an overview, and warns that allergen tags are set by people. Suggestions from the name are pre-ticked only for unreviewed ingredients, say so in amber, and are saved only when a person saves, so a deliberate "none" is never overridden. Older databases upgrade on open. The overview chips filter the list: `/ingredients?show=no-calories` or `?show=unchecked` (links, no scripting; the active chip clears it; counts stay overall; unknown values show all).
- **Recipes (piece 3, built 2026-10-30).** A Recipe is a name, servings, meal types, weather (any, warm, cold), free tags and ingredient lines for the whole Recipe. Calories per serving, allergens and diet badges (pescatarian, vegetarian, vegan) are never typed in: they are computed from the Ingredients each time the page is shown, so reviewing an Ingredient updates every Recipe using it. **A Recipe with any unreviewed Ingredient shows "Not checked" with the names, and no diet badge, never "safe".** Calories show "Calories unknown" until every Ingredient has them. - **Saved Menus, first slice (built 2026-11-02).** `menu_repository.py` (`MenuRepository`, tables `menus`, `menu_meals`, `menu_portions`) keeps a planned Menu: per Meal the day, slot, Recipe id (set to none with the reason "The recipe was deleted" if the Recipe is deleted) and each Eater's servings (dropped if the Eater is removed); `cooked_on` exists but nothing sets it yet. Calories, names, "Not checked" and the shopping list are never stored: `web_saved_menus.py` works them out from the live Recipes, Eaters and Stock (`planner.shopping_for`). The Menu page has "Save this menu" (optional name; `POST /menu/save` plans again from the same settings and seed, so the saved Menu is exactly what was shown), and `/menus` lists saved Menus with `/menus/{id}` and a delete. `web_menu.py` now has `parse_plan`/`PlanRequest` shared by the page and the save. **Swapping a Meal:** each Meal on a saved Menu has a Change link to `/menus/{id}/meals/{meal_id}`, which lists the Recipes tagged for that slot that suit everyone at that Meal (`planner.choices_for`, the same allergy/diet rules; Cooldown and Shopping Allowance are left to the person) with their calories and each person's new servings (`planner.portions_for`, the day's calories shared between the slots on that day of the Menu). Choosing one (`MenuRepository.set_meal`) re-checks the posted Recipe against the same rules and answers 400 if it does not suit, so an id cannot be forced in. **Cooked and Cooldown:** every Meal with a Recipe on a saved Menu has **Mark cooked** (records today, `MenuRepository.set_cooked`) and, once cooked, "Cooked <date>" with **Not cooked** to undo it. A cooked Meal cannot be changed (Change link hidden, a swap answers 400); a Meal with no Recipe cannot be cooked. `MenuRepository.last_served()` is each Recipe's latest cooked date across all saved Menus and is passed to the planner as `last_served` for every plan, including the Menu page and Save, so the Cooldown now works across Menus. A Menu that was only planned or saved, not cooked, does not start a Cooldown. **Cooking uses up Stock (2026-11-02):** `cooking.py` (pure) works out `uses_for(recipe, scale, held)`: the Recipe's amounts times the servings cooked divided by the Recipe's servings, one `Use` per line (a group of alternatives uses one: the first that covers the whole amount, else the one with most in Stock, else the first). Mark cooked takes that from Stock with `StockRepository.use_available` (earliest expiry first, never fails for lack of Stock) and keeps what Stock could not cover in `menu_meals.short`, shown as "Short in stock: carrots 100 g". Cooking never blocks. Pressing Mark cooked again does nothing (stock is taken once). **Not cooked puts Stock back:** cooking records each lot it took (`StockRepository.take_available` returns `stock.TakenLot`s: ingredient, base-unit amount, location, expiry; kept in `menu_meal_taken` by `MenuRepository.set_cooked(..., took=)`), and marking the meal not cooked returns exactly those lots with `StockRepository.put_back` (merged into a matching item, or a new one, without changing what adding remembers), then forgets them, so pressing it again puts nothing back. Only what was really taken goes back, not what was short. A meal cooked before this was added has no record and gives nothing back. The button asks first. **Expired stock never counts:** cooking leaves stock that has expired by today alone (`use_available(..., on=day)`) and reports it as short, and the shopping list and the planner ignore lots that have expired by the meal's day. **The shopping list now has amounts** (`cooking.shopping_amounts`): what the meals still to cook need less what is in Stock, with Stock shared out between the meals in date order and cooked meals ignored; used on both the Menu page and saved Menus (`web_menu.shopping_text`). **The planner works with amounts (2026-11-02):** `cooking.Pantry` holds Stock as (quantity, expiry) lots per Ingredient id; `plan(stock=...)` takes lots, or just a date/None for an amount that is not tracked (always enough until then, which keeps older callers working). A Recipe's Ingredient is covered when what is held and unexpired on the day is at least what the Meal needs at the servings that table would eat (a group of alternatives is covered by any one member), and a chosen Meal uses its share up (`uses_for`) so the next Meal sees less. Uncovered Ingredients count once each against the Shopping Allowance and are assumed bought for the rest of the Menu. Expiring-soon is lots that run out within 7 days and are still usable. Not built yet: reshuffling a single Meal, accepting a Menu to reserve Stock, marking a Meal cooked (deducting Stock), and feeding cooked dates back as `last_served`.
- **Bulk review of ingredients (built 2026-10-30).** `/ingredients/review` replaces opening ingredients one by one, and is built to make a long list short. It lists every unreviewed ingredient (most used in recipes first) in three kinds. **Plain-looking** (`ingredients.judge`: a name made only of basic plant foods and describing words, such as garlic, salt, olive oil, strawberries, jasmine rice, never a bare "oil", never with a hint of an allergen or food group; bracket notes like "(counted)" are ignored and accents are folded): one group of ticked boxes confirmed in one click and saved as reviewed with nothing ticked. **Groups that share a likely answer**: ingredients whose name suggests the same food groups and allergens (at least two) form a group such as "Probably dairy, milk (24)" with its own **Confirm the ticked ones** button, which saves those tags for the ticked members (untick a vegan version, say). **One by one**: everything left, 20 per page, with the name's suggestions pre-ticked, the reason it needs a look, optional calories and a "Looks right" box that ticks itself when anything in the row changes. "Need a look" is the risky filter (`?show=risky` hides the plain group). **Nothing is ever saved by itself**: an ingredient counts as reviewed only when a person confirms it. The server re-checks that ids posted as plain still look plain, skips ingredients already reviewed (never overwrites a person's review), validates everything first and saves nothing if a value is bad (the error names the ingredient and typed values stay), and rebuilds the return address from `show`, `recipe` and a saved count only. `web_ingredient_review.py` is registered before `/ingredients/{id}`, and its POST opens its own `StockRepository` because it is an async route. Typical contents the name suggests (all only hints): flour, oats, granola, muesli, cereal, crackers and pasta shapes mean gluten (not cornflour, rice, almond or coconut flour, nor "gluten-free"); wine, vinegar, balsamic, sherry and dried fruit mean sulphites; worcestershire means fish; mayonnaise means eggs (not vegan); stock, broth and bouillon mean celery; hummus means sesame; hard cheeses mean milk. Not built yet: calories for ingredients in bulk (only optional per row), and dropping non-ingredient lines such as "base recipe" at import.
- **One food, one ingredient, plain names (built 2026-10-30).** `names.name_key(name)` is what two spellings of one food share: case, hyphens, plurals, describing words (sea, kosher, fine, finely, flaky, table, coarse, fresh, freshly, cracked, chopped, diced, sliced, minced, grated, shredded, crushed, roughly, thinly, lightly, organic, ripe, raw, large, small, medium, extra, virgin; "ground" for spices but not for minced meat; "grain" after fine or coarse), a seasoning pair counting as salt ("salt and pepper", "sea salt and black pepper"), and a bare "pepper" meaning black pepper. Colours, "dried" and container words (can, tin, jar) are kept, so red and black pepper, dried and fresh herbs, and tinned and fresh tomatoes stay apart. `names.clean_name(name)` is the plain name stored: describing words dropped, every salt is "salt", every black pepper "black pepper", hyphens kept in real compounds ("gluten-free", "all-purpose") and removed where they join a describing word ("sea-salt", "extra-virgin"). `canonical_name(name, known, family)` reuses a known ingredient with the same key and the same kind of unit (weight, volume or count), else uses the clean name. `StockRepository.add`, `use` and `change_base_unit`, `RecipeRepository` saves and the recipe import all go through it, so inputs are cleaned and a variant never creates a twin. For names already stored: `StockRepository.tidy_plan()` lists the groups (same key and unit kind) with the plain name each would get, `tidy()` carries it out, and `merge_ingredients(keep_id, drop_id)` does one merge (recipe lines and stock move over, quantities add up where a recipe had both, allergens and food groups of both are kept as the safer union, calories keep the kept one's else the other's, reviewed if either was, then the duplicate is deleted; different kinds of unit raise `IncompatibleUnitError`). A rename is skipped if its target name is already used by an ingredient in another kind of unit, so two groups never end up with one name. The Ingredients page shows "N ingredient names can be tidied" with a **See what would change** list and a **Tidy the names** button (`POST /ingredients/tidy`, registered before `/ingredients/{id}`): a person presses it, nothing is tidied by itself. On the 322 imported ingredients it plans 40 changes that take the list to about 300, for example salt, sea salt, fine salt, kosher salt, salt and pepper and sea salt and black pepper all become "salt", and black pepper with freshly cracked black pepper becomes "black pepper" (ground black pepper stays apart there because it is stored by volume).
